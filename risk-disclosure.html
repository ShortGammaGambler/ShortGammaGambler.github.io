2. Options and 0DTE risk

Options involve a high degree of risk and are not suitable for every investor. Key risks include:

  • Total loss of premium. Long options can expire worthless, and short options can be assigned.
  • Leverage. Small moves in the underlying can produce large percentage losses. Uncovered short positions can lose more than the funds in your account.
  • 0DTE and short-dated contracts. Same-day and next-day expiries can move from profit to total loss within minutes, and their pricing is dominated by gamma and pin risk rather than by any directional thesis.
  • Liquidity and spreads. Quotes may be wide, stale, or absent. Market orders can fill far from the displayed price, and stop orders do not guarantee an execution price.
  • Assignment, exercise, and expiration mechanics. Pin risk, early assignment on short American-style options, and automatic exercise thresholds can create unintended stock or futures positions, including overnight and weekend exposure.
  • Margin and house rules. Your broker may liquidate positions without notice. Margin requirements can change intraday.
  • Gaps and halts. Overnight gaps, trading halts, index rebalances, and macro events can render your risk plan unusable.

Only trade with risk capital you can afford to lose entirely, and size positions so that being wrong is survivable.

3. Model output is an estimate

Gamma exposure (GEX), Vanna, and Charm figures are model estimates, not observations. They are derived from third-party options chains and from assumptions about dealer positioning — most commonly the assumption that dealers are short gamma and long vanna or charm in aggregate. Those assumptions can be wrong at the exact moments that matter, including around large expiries and volatility shocks. Open-interest based exposure is inherently backwards-looking: it describes positions that existed, not positions that will be defended.

Consequences to keep in mind:

  • A gamma flip level, zero-gamma point, or charm-weighted drift estimate is a description of a model, not a level the market is obliged to respect.
  • Scanner scores, regime classifications (including any hidden-Markov or clustering label), toxicity indicators, and probabilities are heuristics. They are not forecasts and carry no stated or implied accuracy.
  • Model output is not validated for your account size, holding period, borrow costs, tax position, or execution venue.
  • Errors propagate: bad inputs, stale chains, or a wrong contract multiplier produce confidently wrong output. Verify anything you trade against your broker's own data.

6. Position sizing and the Kelly module

Kelly-criterion and fractional-Kelly outputs depend entirely on the win-rate and payoff estimates fed into them. Those inputs are estimates, and Kelly sizing is mathematically aggressive: it maximises long-run growth only when the inputs are correct, and it produces severe drawdowns when they are not. We therefore present sizing figures as illustrations of a method, not as advice to risk a specific percentage of your capital. A negative or undefined fraction means the method cannot be applied — it does not mean a position is safe in reverse.
